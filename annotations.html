Things That Run Across the Whole Site

These aren't tied to any one page — they're the foundations. Getting these right first will make everything else land better.

📱

It needs to work properly on a phone

Most patients will use this site on their phones — checking fees on the way to an appointment, looking up a number in a hurry. The new site needs to feel just as good on a small screen as it does on a desktop, from the very start.

🎨

The site needs to feel like one thing, not many things

Right now different pages feel like they were built at different times — because they probably were. Deciding on two fonts, a few brand colours, and consistent spacing, then applying them everywhere, would pull the whole site together in a way that's immediately noticeable.

♿

Make sure everyone can use it

Some patients will have low vision, use a screen reader, or struggle with small text. Making sure the site has good contrast, clear labels, and works with a keyboard doesn't take much effort — and it means nobody gets left out.

⚡

Slow pages are genuinely frustrating — especially for unwell patients

If someone's looking up symptoms or a phone number in a hurry, a slow-loading page is the last thing they need. Optimising images and cutting down on heavy embeds (those Google Maps in particular) would make a noticeable difference to how the site feels.

🔍

People searching for a GP nearby should be finding you

Searches like "GP near Silverdale" or "doctor Millwater" are happening every day. With a bit of attention to how the site describes itself to Google, you'd be showing up for exactly those searches — from people actively looking for a practice like yours.

🗺️

The footer shows up on every single page — it's worth getting right

Every patient who scrolls to the bottom of any page sees this. Right now it's a bit crowded and hard to read. A clean footer with proper spacing and a single locations link instead of four maps would feel much more considered.

📞

The phone number should be impossible to miss

For a medical practice, the phone number is arguably the most important thing on the whole site. It should be right at the top, on every page, big enough to tap easily on a phone. If someone needs to call urgently, they shouldn't have to go looking for it.

🔐

A privacy policy needs to be easy to find

Patients are sharing health information with the practice — they should be able to find your Privacy Policy without hunting for it. It's a legal requirement, but more than that, it's just good for trust to show people their information is looked after properly.

🌐

Make sure the padlock is always showing

Patients notice when a site isn't secure — especially when it's a health website. Keeping HTTPS running properly across every page, with a current certificate, is one of those things that just needs to be done. It's table stakes for any health site.

Where to Start

Not everything needs to happen at once. Here's a rough sense of what to tackle first, what comes next, and what can wait.

🔴 Do these first — quick wins that matter

  • Phone number in the header, big and tappable
  • Replace the footer maps with one locations link
  • Add "last reviewed" dates to Fees and Health Alerts
  • Give the action bar buttons (Book / Scripts / Pay) some visual weight
  • Add a booking button to every page
  • Fix text contrast where it's hard to read

🟠 These make the biggest difference

  • New navigation — sticky, mobile-friendly, with one clear CTA
  • Real photography in the hero — this changes everything
  • GP and team profiles on the About page
  • News page with proper cards and images
  • Fees page with anchor links and an enrolment explainer
  • Testimonials and accreditation badges somewhere visible

🟢 Once the core is sorted

  • Consistent design across every page — fonts, colours, spacing
  • Careers page with proper job cards and a culture section
  • Specialist cards with photos and consistent bios
  • Patient Resources redesigned as categorised cards
  • Newsletter signup on the News page
  • SEO housekeeping — page titles, descriptions, structured data

🔵 The exciting stuff — when you're ready

  • Online appointment booking through a patient portal
  • Repeat prescription requests online
  • A proper health library patients can actually browse
  • An interactive locations map page
  • Resources for the team internally
  • Multilingual support — especially for Pacific communities
